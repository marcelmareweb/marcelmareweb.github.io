title: Identity before tools.
evidenceType: Workshop / Reported and observed outcomes
context: One workshop session in 2024 with the National Film and Video Foundation (NFVF) and QuiteFrankly, for a small group of under 20 emerging young Black creatives.
role: Marcel Maré co-designed the session and led it as research facilitator.
question: How can emerging creatives use AI without losing their sense of authorship?
challenge: Generative AI is changing how people enter the creative industries. For emerging creatives it offers new tools, and a risk that their own voice and their employability are diluted. Few had structured support to use AI in a way that strengthened their practice.
investigation: A reflective survey before the session asked how participants saw AI in relation to authorship and identity. In the session, facilitated discussion, observation and the work participants made with the tools showed where confidence and skills fell short.
designedExperience: 'Three principles shaped the session. Identity before tools: reflection anchored AI use in each person’s own creative story. Structured experimentation: Figma, Midjourney, ChatGPT, RunwayML and Jitter were used on defined creative challenges, not explored at random. Translation to employability: the work was reframed as portfolio pieces with a stated design rationale. Live demonstration, co-creation, critique and reflection carried it.'
reported: Participants said they felt more confident using AI tools, had a clearer sense of their creative identity and saw more clearly where they might fit in the industry.
observed: Less hesitation in trying the tools, more peer feedback, clearer descriptions of their own creative value, and portfolio-ready pieces by the end of the session.
uncertain: This was one session with a small group. It shows how people engaged on the day, not whether their confidence or practice changed afterwards. It concerns AI in creative learning, not how organisations govern AI decisions.
