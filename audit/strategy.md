## The short version

The brand doesn't need a new idea. It has one, and the homepage hides it under too much explanation.

The idea is **formation**. Keep the opening, the monkey, the look and the three disciplines. Edit the homepage by cutting, bring the essay voice back, and let the evidence tell its story.

The website is also not what's limiting traction. A first paid pilot is.

## Where the first plan went wrong

- **I audited the site, not the practice.** I hadn't read the essays or the portfolio when I scored Proof at 3/10.
- **I applied a five-second test to a sale that isn't made in five seconds.** The commercial plan is to reach clients "through existing relationships" at about R145,000 for two days [V: Raykis document, pricing marked unvalidated]. That buyer arrives half-interested, wanting a reason to keep reading.
- **I replaced the thesis with a poster.** "More possibilities. Harder choices." names what a leader is living through, and invites you in. "Make a choice. Own its consequences." tells a senior person what to do, and closes the conversation.
- **I treated the brand's two most distinctive assets as clutter:** formation and the monkey.
- **I optimised what I could count.** A 200-word target is exactly what *The Laboratory Fallacy* warns against: "The metrics tell us that the system is working. They cannot tell us whether the system is worth operating."

Much of this came from the brief's hypothesis, but testing it was my job, and I passed the parts that mattered most.

## What's actually going on

### 1. One thesis runs through 25 years of work

**What's possible doesn't settle what people will do.**

| Where | In its own words [V] |
|---|---|
| Cookstove research, as summarised on the thespottedape.com About page | "An available tool does not determine how people will use it." |
| Vantage HIV | "the availability of medication (ARVs and PrEP) is only half the battle" |
| Creative × AI | "Identity Before Tools" |
| *The Deaf Leading the Blind* | "the gap between performed and actual perception" |
| *The Laboratory Fallacy* | "Everything works. Nothing delights." |
| About page | "What enables people to move from knowing what is possible to actually doing something worthwhile?" |
| **Homepage** | **"More possibilities. Harder choices."** |

The opening stays because it's the shortest statement of everything else on this list.

### 2. The name is the working method

The essay on the *Spotted Ape*, a polka-dotted B-24 assembly ship, already sets out what the practice does:

- **The problem:** "Everyone is flying, but no one is forming."
- **The role:** "Assembly ships did not command; they demonstrated."
- **The exit:** "Leadership that lingers breeds dependence."

That's a rare thing for an independent adviser: a brand story in which leaving is the point. The About page already says it ("Good guidance eventually becomes unnecessary"), and the project's own specification defines the term: "A formation is a set of commitments, dependencies, exclusions and unresolved questions." That's an ownable description of strategy.

The problem it solves is also on the About page, and it's sharper than anything in my plan: "capable teams can pursue conflicting priorities without recognising the disagreement."

The monkey follows the same logic. The aircraft worked because it was "absurd and brilliant", in "clownish colours". A bright yellow ape on a severe black-and-white page is the same move. Among firms that call themselves "Applied Behavioural Science Consulting", it's the one thing a visitor will remember [A].

### 3. The voice went missing

The essays are funny, surprising and well read. The site reads as if a compliance team edited it.

| The essays | The site |
|---|---|
| "Everyone is flying, but no one is forming." | "Explore strategic decision support" |
| "This KPI is too important for me to allow you to question it." | "Questions & outcomes" |
| "It does not know that glass shatters, nor that wine is liquid." | "Source-labelled evidence, methods and qualified findings." |

**This is the real brand problem.** The person who wrote the left column isn't on the website. That's also the best answer to creating curiosity, and it needs no photograph or redesign. Keep the honesty; lose the procedural tone.

### 4. The evidence is stronger than I said

The homepage reduces the richest story available to "Cookstove adoption." and a finding. The story is much better than that: co-creative workshops in Dunoon were built around baking bread, not warnings about danger [V: thesis]. A stove also meant identity, competence and family provision [V: portfolio's account]. And the page openly reports what didn't change.

That last part matters. A sales page saying "no significant reduction in paraffin stove ownership" is the most credible sentence on the site [A]. It's the integrity the practice sells.

### 5. The constraint isn't the website

This is the uncomfortable part.

- **Five site builds or audits in 17 days** (15 October to 1 November, by folder dates, including this one) [V].
- **Several competing opening lines in recent weeks:** "If AI does the work," / "More intelligence. Less direction." / "More possibilities. Harder choices." / "I Help People Think Clearly" / "Better decisions. Not more strategic planning." [V]
- **Last Substack essay: 29 April 2026,** six months ago [V: feed].
- **The flagship is unsold as far as the files show.** Your 17 October note says the next priority is "to move from concept development to a deliverable commercial service", with a paid pilot as step five [V]. I can't see what has happened since.

A pilot produces what no redesign can: a decision-level case, a validated price and a referral. Another redesign, including the one I proposed, is more comfortable than asking someone for R125,000 [A]. It's also the most useful thing this review can tell you.

## Where I differ from the review

I agree with nearly all of it. Two refinements:

- **Founder.** No photo or credentials on the homepage. But buyers of a R145,000 engagement buy a person, and a solo practice that hides who you'd be talking to can read as evasive [A]. So: full treatment on `/about/`, and one plain line on `/contact/` saying who will reply.
- **Curiosity needs a floor.** "A provocative statement can be more effective than a complete explanation" is true only because the promise sits directly beneath it. That sentence must not be cut in the name of intrigue.
