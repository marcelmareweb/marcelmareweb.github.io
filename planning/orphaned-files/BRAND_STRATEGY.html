Brand strategy — The Spotted Ape

Status: Draft for owner review · Date: 6 November 2026 · Owner: Marcel Maré

This document sets out the brand strategy for The Spotted Ape (Pty) Ltd. It builds on the positioning in AGENTS.md and the visual direction in CLAUDE.md. It does not replace either: they remain the authority for positioning, design system and site rules. Where this document proposes a change to them, it says so and waits for approval.

Evidence key. Every claim carries one of these tags:

  • [Supplied]: stated in the repository, the site or ../the-spotted-ape-sources/.
  • [Observed]: seen in the built site on 6 November 2026.
  • [Hypothesis]: a reasoned proposal that still needs testing.
  • [Recommendation]: a choice this document asks the owner to approve.

No client interviews, market sizing, competitor research, trademark searches or recognition tests were carried out. Section 11 lists what should be.

1. The strategy on one page

Who it is for. Leaders and their teams facing a consequential choice that their usual assumptions no longer settle: what to commit to, what to drop, and how people and AI will share the work that follows. [Supplied, narrowed by Hypothesis]

What it offers. An independent guide who structures the decision before effort is committed to a plan. It names the alternatives, tests the assumptions, makes the trade-offs explicit, and then designs for what people will actually do. [Supplied]

Positioning. For leaders who must commit under uncertainty, The Spotted Ape is the independent decision and behavioural design practice that turns a difficult choice into an examined, owned commitment. It does this by making the reasoning visible: alternatives, assumptions, trade-offs, owner and review. Large consultancies tend to deliver a plan, and AI tools tend to deliver more options. The Spotted Ape delivers the choice, and the conditions to act on it. [Recommendation]

Promise. See differently. Choose deliberately. Turn the choice into action. (already on the homepage) [Supplied]

Proof. Three types of evidence, each labelled with its limits:

  • a strategic decision case account (policy analyst, with a reported outcome);
  • an applied learning case (Should We Invest in Solar?);
  • peer-reviewed participatory behavioural research (the cookstove study, Maré, M’Rithaa & Chisin 2023).

[Supplied]

The brand idea. The decision record. The practice shows its method instead of describing it. Every touchpoint is laid out the way the practice lays out a decision. [Supplied as design direction; adopted here as the organising brand idea, Recommendation]

Distinctive codes to own. The spotted mark (a ring with a yellow spot), the yellow signal that marks commitment and nothing else, period-ended oversized headlines, the choice control, and the formation motif (dispersion becoming a formation). [Observed]

What it will not be. An AI agency, an automation-maturity framework, a generic innovation studio or a source of guaranteed outcomes. [Supplied]

2.2 The most consequential brand problem

The practice has a rare combination but sells it as three separate services. [Hypothesis]

The combination is strategic decision framing, behavioural research credentials and a working method for human–AI accountability. Visitors meet three parallel capabilities and must work out for themselves why they belong to one practice. The site’s own answer, “A real decision rarely fits one capability. The work starts with your question, not a method”, appears late, on the services page.

The consequence is that buyers compare each capability with a specialist: a strategy consultant, a behavioural insights unit, an AI adviser. On any single axis the practice looks smaller than the specialist. On the combined question, “what should we commit to, and will people actually do it?”, it has few direct alternatives. [Hypothesis]

Strategic response. Lead with the decision, not the capabilities. The capabilities are three lenses on one decision: is it the right choice, will people act on it, and who is accountable when AI is involved? Section 4 turns this into positioning and Section 9 into specific copy.

2.3 Tensions to keep explicit

Tension Choice made here
“We” versus one person. The site speaks as “we”, while About presents the practice of one named practitioner. Keep “we” as the practice voice, because the work involves clients, collaborators and co-researchers. Make Marcel visible wherever trust is formed (About, contact, case accounts). Never imply a team or offices that do not exist.
AI relevance versus AI identity. AI drives demand, but AGENTS.md forbids AI-agency positioning. AI is a condition the decision has to account for, not the product. Human–AI Decision Design is about authority and accountability, not tools.
Rigour versus reach. Qualified claims build trust but are less quotable. Keep the qualifications. Make the method the quotable part, not the outcomes.
Distinctive name versus serious category. “The Spotted Ape” is memorable but does not explain itself. Keep it. Pair it with a plain descriptor in every first impression (Section 7.2). The meaning of the name is an open question (Section 12).
Two domains. The current site is on GitHub Pages, while resources live on thespottedape.com. No change is authorised (AGENTS.md). Flagged as an owner decision about findability (Section 9.3).

Doctoral behavioural research with a published article; real course materials; an honest evidence culture; a method visible in the site itself; a distinctive name and mark; a strong, restrained visual system. 2.5 Competitive alternatives

These are the alternatives a buyer may weigh. They are categories, not named firms, because no competitor research was done. [Hypothesis]

Alternative What the buyer gets Where The Spotted Ape differs
Large strategy consultancy Analysis, a recommended plan, implementation capacity Smaller scope; frames the choice and its trade-offs with the leaders who own it; behavioural grounding
Behavioural insights unit or agency Nudges, trials, policy design Starts from the strategic decision, not only the behaviour; designs the decision as well as the intervention
AI consultancy Use cases, tooling, automation Defines decision authority and review points; AI is not the deliverable
Executive education provider Courses and credentials Applied cases built around a real choice; tailored workshops
Facilitator or coach A good conversation A structured record: alternatives, assumptions, owner, review
Doing it internally, or with an AI assistant Speed, low cost, endless options An independent challenger who narrows options and makes commitments explicit

Provisional map (Hypothesis, not data). Axis 1: delivers a plan → delivers an owned choice. Axis 2: treats people as implementers → designs for actual behaviour. The Spotted Ape aims for the owned-choice, actual-behaviour corner. Test this with five to eight buyer interviews before using it externally.

3. The six brand questions

4. Strategic choices

4.1 Primary audience and buying situations

[Hypothesis, to be tested in interviews]

  1. The leader before a commitment. A CEO, director or head of strategy with several credible priorities and one budget. Trigger: a strategy review, a board deadline or a planning cycle that has stalled.
  2. The team whose agreement has not become action. An operations, programme or policy lead whose decision was made but is not being acted on. Trigger: an adoption or uptake problem.
  3. The organisation bringing AI into consequential work. Trigger: a pilot moving into a live workflow, or a governance question about who can approve, challenge or stop an action.
  4. The learner or the learning buyer. An L&D lead or an individual seeking applied strategic judgement. Trigger: a programme brief, or a search for a practical case.

Situations 1 to 3 are the core. Situation 4 builds reach and reputation and feeds the core.

4.2 The customer’s job

  • External problem: too many credible options, and plans that do not survive contact with how people work.
  • Internal problem: the fear of committing to the wrong thing, or of looking indecisive while reviewing.
  • Philosophical problem: consequential choices should be examined, owned and reviewable, not inherited or automated by default.

[Hypothesis, derived from site copy]

4.3 Positioning statement

For leaders who must commit under uncertainty, The Spotted Ape is the independent decision and behavioural design practice that turns a difficult choice into an examined, owned commitment, because it makes the reasoning visible and designs for what people will actually do. Large consultancies and AI tools add options and plans; we help you choose, and act. [Recommendation]

Onliness test. The Spotted Ape is the only practice that joins strategic decision design with published behavioural research and human–AI accountability, for leaders in Southern Africa and beyond.

The word “only” is not substantiated. Use this sentence internally to sharpen focus, never in public copy. [Hypothesis]

Customer transformation. From a stalled choice among plausible priorities to a framed decision with explicit trade-offs, a named owner and a review point, through structured work with an independent guide.

4.4 Message pillars and reasons to believe

Pillar Claim Proof (with its limit)
Choose Examine the decision before committing more effort to a plan. The policy-analyst case account (reported outcome, not typical); the decision record; the Strategy Illusion map
Act Begin with what people actually do. The cookstove study: published, with qualified findings and no claim of lasting adoption
Own Make clear where people decide, review, challenge or stop. The masterclass Steward day; Find Formation. Gap: no Human–AI case account yet
Learn by doing From knowledge to judgement to action. The Solar course and its real materials (describes the learner’s task, makes no outcome claim)

4.5 What we will not do or say

  • Not an AI agency. No automation-maturity ladders, no !Kharos Strategy, no Agentic Mastery. [Supplied]
  • No guaranteed outcomes, no percentages, and no claims that AI validates strategies or predicts reliably. [Supplied]
  • No public cohort dates, seat prices, cart or checkout until they are real. [Supplied]
  • No invented team, offices, logos of clients or testimonials.
  • No competing on breadth, scale, implementation headcount or speed for its own sake.
  • No borrowing from Huge beyond structure and typography. [Supplied]

6. Narrative

6.1 ABT (the core story)

Organisations have more options than ever, and AI keeps adding more. But more possibilities make choosing harder, and plans stall, or are made and never acted on. Therefore we help leaders examine the decision itself (alternatives, assumptions, trade-offs, owner and review) and design for what people will actually do.

6.2 StoryBrand outline

Element Content
Character A leader with a consequential choice and a team that has to carry it out
Problem Too many plausible priorities; assumptions that no longer hold; agreement that does not become action
Guide Empathy: “When conditions turn chaotic, speed is not your friend.” Authority: doctoral behavioural research, an applied course, case accounts
Plan 1. Bring the question. 2. Examine alternatives and assumptions together. 3. Leave with a decision, an owner and a review point.
Call to action Direct: Let’s talk. Transitional: try Find Formation, read a decision map, open the Solar case
Failure Effort committed to an unexamined plan; capable teams pulling in conflicting directions
Success Movement with direction: a formation, not a dispersion

There is no villain. The antagonist is a condition (uncertainty, overload), never a competitor or a person.

6.3 The brand’s own story

The About page already tells the backstory well: from why households adopt new energy technologies to how organisations navigate strategic choices. Keep it as one sentence of lineage, not a biography. The formation motif carries the moral: movement needs direction.

7. Verbal identity

7.1 Voice

Trait Means Not
Plain Short sentences, everyday words, one idea per sentence. “Several priorities. One commitment.” Simplistic, or slogan without substance
Exact Says what was observed and what was not. Labels evidence by type. Hedged into vagueness
Questioning Leads with the question that matters. “What needs deciding?” Coy, or answering every question with a question
Calm Unhurried, no urgency tricks, no superlatives. Cold or academic
Committed Ends with a choice, an owner or a next step. Headlines end in a period. Pushy

Writing rules.

  • Headlines are short, end in a period and break deliberately. [Supplied]
  • Use sentence case; British spelling; round quotes; the en dash in “Human–AI”.
  • Name the evidence type beside the claim (“Case account / Reported outcome”).
  • Use the reader’s language: choice, commitment, trade-off, assumption, owner, review.

Banned words and moves. Unlock, empower, leverage, transform (unqualified), cutting-edge, innovative, world-class, AI-powered, synergy, journey (for a sales funnel), “only”, “best”, “guaranteed”, percentages without a source, fake urgency.

7.2 Descriptor and name

  • Descriptor: Strategic decision and behavioural design, Cape Town. Pair it with the name in the page title, the meta description, social cards, email signatures and proposals. [Supplied: already in the homepage title]
  • One-sentence description: The Spotted Ape helps leaders make consequential choices when familiar assumptions no longer hold. [Supplied]
  • Elevator (about 40 words): We help leaders choose when there are too many good options. We frame the decision, test the assumptions behind each alternative and design for what people will actually do, including where AI sits in the work. You leave with a choice, an owner and a review point. [Recommendation]

8. Visual identity

The visual system is approved and fully specified in CLAUDE.md. This section only links it to the strategy and names which codes to protect. No redesign is proposed. [Supplied]

8.1 Strategy to design

Strategic idea Design decision
The decision record: show the method The record instrument: one ink rule, then field-and-value rows. Pages are ordered question → alternatives → evidence → invitation.
Choose (commitment) Yellow is reserved for a chosen alternative, the current state and focus. The square choice control marks commitment.
Calm rigour Ink and paper ground (about 85% paper), one family (Inter), generous space, no gradients or shadows.
Exact, with labelled evidence Field labels in --font-label; evidence-type tags on cards; media made from the item’s own content, never decoration.
Movement needs direction The formation motif: About’s orientation field, Find Formation, the intro’s flights of planes.

8.2 Distinctive brand assets

Asset Status Rule
The spot: a yellow dot on a ring (favicon) Strong, underused Make it the recurring signature: avatar, social cards, slide corners, sign-off. Its single meaning is the point of decision. [Recommendation]
Wordmark “THE SPOTTED APE” In use Inter 600, 15px, +0.06em tracking, one unbroken line. Do not restyle.
Ape mark In use Keep as approved. Do not redraw it without a separate brief.
Yellow #FFFF00 as signal Strong Accent only. Its scarcity is what makes it a code. Never a background band, never text on white.
Period-ended oversized headlines Strong Every headline, every channel, including slides and social posts.
Choice control (square, ink border, yellow when chosen) Unique within the system Use wherever the reader commits: exercises, polls, workshop materials.
Formation / flights Emerging Use for transitions between dispersion and direction. Keep it abstract (lines, nodes, planes), never illustrative.
2×2 decision maps Emerging A recurring thinking format. Same grid, same label positions, same signal use.

Swap test. Would the asset still work for a generic consultancy? The ink-and-white palette and Inter alone would, so they are not distinctive on their own. The spot, the yellow-as-commitment rule, the choice control and the formation motif would not. Invest in those. [Hypothesis; fame and uniqueness are untested]

[Recommendation]

9.2 Audit findings

Each finding uses the format observation → evidence → consequence → fix → priority.

  1. The capabilities read as three services, not one practice.
    • Evidence: homepage and How we help. The integrating line comes last on the services page. [Observed]
    • Consequence: buyers compare each capability with a specialist.
    • Fix: move “A real decision rarely fits one capability. The work starts with your question, not a method.” to the top of How we help, directly under the headline. Frame the three capabilities as three questions about one decision: Is it the right choice? Will people act on it? Who answers for it? [Recommendation]
    • Priority: High · Confidence: Hypothesis
  2. The strategic case evidence is thin.
    • Evidence: one case account in src/content/cases/. [Observed]
    • Consequence: the Choose pillar rests on a single story, and the Human–AI capability has no case at all.
    • Fix: write two or three more case accounts (anonymised and cleared) using the existing format: context, shift, reported outcome, limits. Prioritise one Human–AI engagement.
    • Priority: High
  3. Split web presence.
    • Evidence: the site is on marcelmareweb.github.io; resources are on thespottedape.com; the biography is on marcelmare.com. [Supplied]
    • Consequence: weaker findability, and the brand name and URL disagree.
    • Fix: an owner decision. AGENTS.md currently forbids a custom domain, so no change is made here. Before any migration, the uppercase /Courses/ and research archives must be preserved.
    • Priority: High (decision) · Confidence: Supported
  4. The name is not explained.
    • Evidence: no reference to the name’s meaning in the repo or sources. [Observed]
    • Consequence: a memorable name with no story behind it.
    • Fix: supply the origin. If it is meaningful, add one sentence to About. If not, let the descriptor carry the meaning.
    • Priority: Medium
  5. Inconsistent cue on the decision-map card.
    • Evidence: WorkStrip.astro cue reads “Design Strategy.”, but the card rules say “Explore.” for maps. [Observed]
    • Fix: use “Explore.”
    • Priority: Low
  6. The solar card media text repeats its title, and the title has no period.
    • Evidence: WorkStrip.astro. [Observed]
    • Fix: either use the course’s actual question as the media text (for example “Which of three solar proposals should Cape Manufacturing fund?”), or keep the image only. Either way, title the card “Should we invest in solar.” or keep the course name as a proper title, and decide which convention wins for named courses.
    • Priority: Low
  7. The Human–AI question in the data has no question mark and a different wording.
    • Evidence: src/data/practice.ts:50: “Where must people review, challenge and develop resilient approaches”. [Observed]
    • Fix: “Where must people decide, review, challenge or stop an action?”
    • Priority: Low
